Why This Work Exists

Most problems in high-stakes work are not strategic first. They are perceptual.

You do not lose composure because the stakes are high. You lose it because specific people distort how you see, interpret, and respond. This work exists to address that distortion — and it holds up just as well worked in a room of peers as it does one on one.

The Problem This Solves

Specific people are costing you composure.

Not in theory. In depositions, negotiations, and boardrooms. The mastermind works through six of these relationships, one a month, with a group that's carrying the same kind of charge — just not toward the same person.

The Adversary

Opposing counsel, the counterparty, the negotiator across the table. Their provocation is deliberate — and it works. You leave still arguing with them in your head, and your strategy narrows to a reaction.

The Client

They ignore your counsel, then hold you responsible for the outcome. Resentment creeps into the relationship, and your judgment stops being clean.

The Boss or Senior Partner

Their scrutiny changes your posture. You over-prepare, second-guess, and start performing competence instead of exercising it.

The Board

Every meeting becomes a firing squad. One member's hostility triggers your defensiveness before the agenda even starts — and the whole room reads it.

The Rival

A peer competing for the same ground — the promotion, the account, the mandate. Their presence pulls you off your own game and into theirs.

The Direct Report

Their resistance pulls you toward micromanagement, avoidance, or frustration. Delegation becomes distorted by charge, and the work suffers for it.

What the Mastermind Delivers

Structured work. One relationship a month.

Cohort Facilitation

What this is — and is not. The Demartini Method is a structured perceptual-reframing facilitation: a systematic sequence of written and guided questions designed to bring balance to how you perceive a specific person. It is educational and developmental in nature, delivered here in a small group format. It does not diagnose, treat, or provide therapy for any medical, psychological, or mental health condition, and it is not group therapy or a substitute for care from a licensed mental health professional. If you are dealing with a mental health condition, please work with a qualified provider.

Before You Apply

The two objections worth naming.

Nobody considers doing this work in a group without wondering about these two things first.

“Will I have to share my personal business with strangers?”

No. You are never asked to name the person, describe the relationship, or disclose what happened. The facilitation runs on your own private worksheet — the group works the same sequence in parallel, not out loud.

Cohorts are also built across industries and firms. No two people from competing practices or the same matter are ever placed in the same group, so there is no live conflict of interest sitting in the room with you.

“Isn't a mastermind too generic to work on my specific situation?”

A typical mastermind is a roundtable — advice, accountability, shared war stories. This is not that. It follows the exact structured Demartini sequence used in the private advisory, applied to your own charged relationship, every session.

The difference from private facilitation is who's in the room, not the rigor of the method. What changes is the price, and the company you do it in.

Common Questions

What to expect.

How is this different from a typical mastermind or peer group?▼

Most masterminds are roundtables — members trade advice and hold each other accountable to goals. This one follows a structured facilitation method, the same one used in private sessions, applied to your own material every month. The group provides the cadence and the company; it doesn't dilute the rigor.

Will I have to share personal or case details with the group?▼

No. You're never asked to name the person or describe the situation out loud. The facilitation happens on your own private worksheet, guided live, while the rest of the cohort works the same sequence on their own material in parallel.

How is confidentiality and conflict of interest handled within a cohort?▼

Cohorts are built across industries and firms — no two members from the same firm, opposing sides of a matter, or directly competing practices are placed together. Everyone in the group signs a confidentiality agreement as a condition of the engagement. To be clear: this is not a legally privileged setting, and the method is deliberately structured so no privileged or case-identifying information is ever required.

What's the cohort size, and how are members selected?▼

Cohorts run 8–12 members (6 for the Executive Cohort). Applications are reviewed individually to confirm fit and to check for overlap with anyone already enrolled before a seat is confirmed.

Is this therapy or mental health treatment?▼

No. The Demartini Method is a structured, educational facilitation process, not psychotherapy, counseling, or group therapy. It does not diagnose or treat any medical, psychological, or mental health condition, and it is not a substitute for care from a licensed mental health professional.

Will this blunt my edge in an adversarial setting?▼

No. Aggression that you choose is a tool; aggression that gets pulled out of you is a tell. The work removes the second without touching the first — your intensity becomes something you deploy deliberately rather than something opposing counsel can trigger on demand.

What if I miss a session?▼

Each session is recorded for members, and your private workbook lets you complete that month's relationship on your own timeline. Two consecutive missed live sessions typically prompt a short catch-up call to keep you on pace with the cohort.

Can I move from the mastermind into the private advisory later?▼

Yes. Members who want deeper, one-on-one work on a specific relationship can move into the private advisory at any point, and a portion of mastermind fees paid within the prior six months applies toward the first advisory engagement.

Do I need to have attended one of your keynotes?▼

No. Many members first encountered the work during a keynote, but the mastermind is open to any professional dealing with charge toward specific people.
