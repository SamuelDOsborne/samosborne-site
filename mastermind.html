The Curriculum

The six-month study.

Each session works one stage of the transcendent function, in sequence, applied live to your own material.

  1. I

    Reading a Dream as Data

    Moving from "what does this mean" to a disciplined method of amplification and personal association.

  2. II

    Locating the Opposites

    Finding the genuine tension underneath a symptom, a recurring image, or a stuck decision.

  3. III

    Active Imagination

    Holding both poles in imaginal contact, without collapsing toward either one, until something moves.

  4. IV

    The Transcendent Function

    Where the third position — genuinely new, not a compromise — begins to appear.

  5. V

    Nigredo, Albedo, Rubedo

    The alchemical stages of the work, and how to recognize which one you're standing in.

  6. VI

    Mercurius and the Symbol That Won't Resolve

    What to do with material that refuses a tidy ending — and why that's often the point.

Common Questions

What to expect.

What if I don't remember my dreams?+

Recall is a skill that develops with the practice, not a prerequisite for it. The curriculum works just as well with a waking symbol or a decision you can't resolve rationally.

Is this therapy or mental health treatment?+

No. This is an educational study group, not psychotherapy or medical care. It's not a substitute for licensed treatment.

How is this different from a book club?+

A book club discusses the text. This applies it — live, structured facilitation on material a member brings, using amplification and active imagination.

What's the group size and confidentiality?+

Cohorts are capped at twelve (six for the Inner Circle). Everyone agrees to hold what's shared as confidential.

Do I need a background in Jung to join?+

No. The reading guides carry the terminology; you bring the material and the willingness to sit with it.

What is the Demartini Method, and how does it fit with the Jungian work?+

The Demartini Method is a structured, ledger-based technique for locating and balancing opposites — it's the entry point each session uses to surface the material cleanly. From there, the work moves into Jungian active imagination, where a genuinely new third position is allowed to emerge, rather than a compromise between the two sides.
