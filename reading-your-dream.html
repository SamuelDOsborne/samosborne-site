The method

The Magnum Opus. Your great work of recording the soul.

The alchemists called their life’s work the magnum opus, the Great Work. Jung saw in it a mirror of individuation: the lifelong process of becoming whole. He kept his own record, first in the Black Books and then, for some sixteen years, in the painted folio we now call the Red Book.

“The years … when I pursued the inner images, were the most important time of my life.”C. G. Jung, 1957

Why dreams

2,500 years of taking dreams seriously.

From the Logos of Heraclitus to the sleep lab, the same idea keeps returning: the soul has an order of its own, and the night is where it speaks.

Heraclitus · c. 500 BCEThe Logos

The soul has a depth no road can reach.

“You will not find the boundaries of soul by traveling in any direction, so deep is the measure [logos] of it.” And: “The waking have one common world, but the sleeping turn aside each into a world of his own.”

Your dream world is the one place only you can enter.

Plato · c. 385 BCEThe Symposium · “Know thyself”

The search for your other half is ancient.

In Plato’s Symposium, Aristophanes imagines humans split in two, forever searching for their missing half. At the temple of Apollo at Delphi, the inscription was simpler: know thyself.

The Soulmate Within brings the two together.

C. G. Jung · 1916–1948The Telos of dreams

Dreams have a purpose, not only a cause.

Jung read dreams by their finality: “the immanent psychological striving for a goal.” He saw them compensating for one-sided waking attitudes and sketching the way ahead, as little prophetic, he said, as a weather forecast.

Ask every dream: what are you for?

Pauli & Jung · 1932–1958Physics meets the psyche

A Nobel physicist recorded his dreams.

Wolfgang Pauli shared about a thousand of his dreams with Jung, who studied some 400 of them. Their 26-year correspondence led to a joint book in 1952, The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche.

Rigorous minds take the night seriously.

Dr. John Demartini · todayThe hidden order

Emotion follows perception.

The Demartini Method is a structured questioning process for balancing perceptions and dissolving emotional charges. Its premise: “every emotion is a by-product of the ratio of perceptions.” Balance the ratio, and the charge lets go.

The method that turns a dream’s message into change.

Walker & van der Helm · 2009REM sleep · emotional memory

Sleep as overnight therapy.

Neuroscientists Matthew Walker and Els van der Helm proposed that REM sleep reprocesses emotional memories in a brain low in stress chemistry, keeping the memory while softening its sting.

Your dreams show what your mind is still working through.

About Sam

Dr. Sam Osborne

Author · keynote speaker · trained Demartini Method Facilitator

Sam’s work rests on one conviction: there is a hidden order inside apparent chaos, and the night is where it shows itself most clearly.

Born in Perth, Australia and now based in Burbank, California, Sam holds a double major in Philosophy and English and has published six books, with a seventh, The Ordered Field, under way. Before all that, he was a teacher, a human beatboxer, a living statue, an events DJ and a kitchen tiler.
